# Narrative outline

## Established premise
Pokemon Patina is an original native GBA ROM hack. Use established community mechanics and backported species where practical; pokeemerald-expansion is the proposed foundation. Industrialism is literal (environmental destruction and exploitation of Pokemon) and metatextual (nostalgia, remakes and remasters extracting value from things no longer appreciated for inherent beauty). Eight rival industries sponsor the region's gyms and offer genuinely useful optional sponsorship to the player. Refusal has no compensating reward and is not intended to prevent normal progression.

## Narrative principles
Industry need not be inherently evil; exploitation and commodification are the targets. Natural Pokemon should exist independently of economic usefulness. Acid is a natural as well as industrial element, not an evil type. Make corporate products appealing enough that the player experiences the tension rather than receiving a lecture. Ordinary people and Pokemon retain agency and value. Corporate propaganda is unreliable; established Pokemon mechanics remain consistent.

## Four intersecting plots — established structure
1. League: defeat eight gyms, challenge Elite Four and become Champion. The conventional journey is a complete primary progression path.
2. Corporations: uncover the truth behind the eight rival industries and decide whether to accept or reject sponsorships. Choices lead to three distinct endings; exact thresholds and consequences TBD. Sponsorship remains optional with real benefits and no compensation for refusal.
3. Legendary crisis: Mew warns the player that environmental destruction and Pokemon exploitation recreate the conditions behind Mewtwo's rage. Mewtwo recruits legendary Pokemon and turns them against humanity, engineers corruption of the Dark/Electric solar Pokemon, and places Celebi in conditions that break its spirit (Grass/Acid corrupted form). Mewtwo is a post-Elite Four antagonist. Legendary hunts and efforts to prevent corruption/recruitment are unlocked through puzzles and exploration chains. The four plots intersect; exact timing, encounter rules and resolution TBD.
4. Criminal organization: a Team Rocket-like organization exploits environmental conditions and commercial culture through bootleg merchandise, Pokemon misinformation, smuggling and related schemes. Name, leadership, corporate relationships and resolution TBD.

Mewtwo exploits real human wrongdoing rather than being the source of all environmental problems (recommended narrative principle). Candidate intersections: corporate mining exposes legendary habitats; smuggling records reveal hidden locations; gym progression opens exploration routes; Mew provides clues to independently investigate legendary recruitment. Post-League Mewtwo conflict reflects legendary quest outcomes through the Legendary Gauntlet (established). Do not require sponsorship to access core story.

## Legendary Gauntlet — established encounter structure
Post-Elite Four, Mewtwo is reached through a gym-like dungeon with consecutive battles against recruited legendary groups (e.g. birds and beasts), using only unresolved/recruited members. If Articuno and Moltres have been successfully dealt with, the birds encounter contains only Zapdos. If all members are resolved, skip that encounter entirely. Complete all remaining battles and Mewtwo in one attempt; a wipe resets all gauntlet battles on return, not permanent legendary quest outcomes. Use ordinary trainer-sized enemy parties rather than requiring an expanded battle party. No Pokemon Center recovery between encounters; leaving voluntarily resetting the run, item restrictions, encounter order, and exact rosters are proposed or TBD. Legendary chains require gathering environmental and NPC clues, general Pokemon knowledge, and satisfying conditions including potentially persuading the legendary in a debate against Mewtwo; merely locating and capturing is insufficient as the entire quest design. See sidequests.md.

## Proposed progression scaffold (not an approved plot)
1. Opening: familiar trainer journey, catching and research; introduce sponsorship as a tempting practical choice.
2. Early gyms: encounter contrasting corporate philosophies, useful services and subtle ecological changes. NPC rumors introduce Eden's Plusle/Minun falsehood.
3. Midgame: show visible habitat loss, contested resources and conflicts between businesses. Evidence of daycare egg swaps and artificial scarcity emerges. Player can investigate without requiring refusal of sponsorship.
4. Late game: Eden's Magikarp capture program becomes visible through public waterways, paid reserves and holding facilities; corporate rivalries expose competing incentives. Heritage's Eevee collection campaign exemplifies nostalgia and mascot favoritism.
5. League/endgame: become Champion, then confront Mewtwo in the post-Elite Four storyline. Three sponsorship-dependent endings are established; details and relationship to legendary quest outcomes TBD.

## Established Eden plot
Eden falsely markets Plusle/Minun as sex variants of first-generation Pikachu and blames trainers for failed evolution. It manipulates daycare eggs to sustain the lie. Its larger project is to remove Magikarp from circulation and manufacture rarity. See [factions](factions-groups-gyms.md) and [sidequests](sidequests.md).
